The Walking Study

Weyand et al. 2010 measured what it costs the body to walk. It found that cost per kilo is set mostly by how tall you are.

The study

48 people aged 5 to 32 walked on a flat treadmill at six speeds while their energy use was measured. The researchers subtracted resting burn, so the result is the cost of walking itself. Walking a distance equal to your height costs about 0.94 calories per kilo of body weight, whoever you are.

Read the study (Journal of Experimental Biology) →

The formula

kcal = 0.94 × weight (kg) × distance ÷ height

Distance and height in the same unit. Result is calories above resting.

Example: 70 kg, 175 cm, 5 km is about 190 kcal.

Read this before you trust it

It counts walking only, not the calories you would burn sitting still, so it reads lower than most watches. The study covered ages 5 to 32 on flat, firm ground, so hills, soft ground, heavy loads and running are not covered.
